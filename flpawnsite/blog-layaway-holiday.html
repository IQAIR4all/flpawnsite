What Is Layaway, and How Does It Work at Florida Pawn?

Layaway is an old-school concept with a lot of modern appeal: you put a deposit on an item, we hold it for you, and you make payments until it's paid off — then you take it home. No financing, no interest, no credit pull. Just a straightforward agreement between you and us.

Pick your item

Browse our in-store inventory at Hollywood or Margate. See something you love — a watch, a piece of jewelry, electronics — and let us know you want to put it on layaway.

Put down a deposit

A small deposit holds the item. We take it off the floor so no one else can buy it while you're paying it off.

Make payments on your schedule

Stop in whenever works for you and make payments toward the balance. There's no rigid payment calendar — just pay it down before the agreed pickup date.

Pick it up when it's paid off

Once the balance is zeroed out, the item is yours. Walk out with it in time for the holidays — no last-minute shipping anxiety, no sold-out disappointments.

Pro tip

The earlier in the season you start, the more time you have to pay it down comfortably. Items put on layaway now give you several weeks of breathing room before the holidays hit.

Why Layaway Makes Sense for Holiday Gifts

Credit card debt from holiday shopping is one of the most common financial regrets people report every January. Holiday spending surveys consistently show the average American puts over $900 on gifts — a significant chunk of which lands on credit cards at 20–30% interest and lingers into the new year. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau recommends avoiding high-interest debt for holiday purchases whenever possible. Layaway sidesteps that trap entirely.

Layaway vs. Buying on Credit: A Real Comparison

Let's say you're buying a pre-owned luxury watch as a gift — priced at $1,200 at Florida Pawn.

Credit Card
$1,200 + interest

At 24% APR, carrying that balance for 6 months adds roughly $85–$120 in interest on top of the purchase price. You're paying more for the same watch.

Retail Financing
$1,200 + fees & credit pull

Many "deferred interest" financing plans charge all accrued interest retroactively if you don't pay in full before the promo period ends — a trap the FTC warns consumers about. And they check your credit.
